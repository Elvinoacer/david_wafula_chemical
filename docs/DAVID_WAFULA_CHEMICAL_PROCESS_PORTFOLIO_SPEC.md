# 78. Success Criteria

A recruiter should quickly understand:

## Who is David?
A Moi University Chemical & Process Engineering graduate.

## What direction is he pursuing?
Process design, simulation, production and plant operations.

## What evidence supports that?
Process-design projects, balances, simulation work, lab exposure and industrial attachment.

## Does he think like a process engineer?
Yes. The portfolio shows system boundaries, streams, balances, unit operations, control, safety and economics.

## Does he understand safety?
Yes, at an appropriate fresh-graduate level.

## Is he overselling himself?
No. Academic work, supervised industrial exposure and professional responsibility are clearly separated.

## Can he be contacted easily?
Yes.

# 79. Final Design Principle

The portfolio should not make David look like an already-established process engineer.

Its strongest message should be:

> **David has strong chemical and process engineering foundations, understands how material, energy and operating conditions interact across a process, can analyze and communicate technical systems clearly, and is ready to grow through real industrial engineering practice.**

Every section should reinforce that story.
